## Reveal and foreshadowing techniques

Carry any reveal in labels, counters, sound and fades, which this design already has, so one text swap can recast the game.

**Technique toolkit**
- Euphemistic labels: change the words, not the mechanic (Severance, Undertale). Beholder's DLC calls euthanasia "Blissful sleep."
- Coded errands: one critic reads Hotline Miami's phone jobs as ordinary tasks hiding kills.
- Numbers for people: tokens carry IDs and a bland running total (Papers, Please; Orwell). Names do the opposite (TLOU2's enemies call each other by name).
- Distance: coarse imagery, delay, muffled aftermath (Unmanned; Spec Ops).
- Tone mismatch: cheerful sound over harm (DDLC's "light, bouncy" score).
- Glitches: start rare and grow; DDLC's anomalies "become commonplace."
- Echo objects: one early prop pays off late (Severance's corridor paintings).
- Motifs: repeat a phrase or sound at fixed points, then recast it (BioShock; Braid's reversed final level flips chaser and chased).
- Second look: same level or batch, new labels (NieR; Undertale's altered dialogue).
- Earned: KOTOR's writers cited The Sixth Sense as the model for enough clues.

**Options for Michael**
- Label layer: all puzzle text in one table, plus a second table for later. Low cost.
- Motif layer: one sound and one icon shared by puzzle success and the laser. Low cost.
- Echo layer: puzzle glyphs reused on Earth props and robot paths. Low cost.
- Second-look layer: optional replay of one batch using the second table. Medium cost.
- Fake errors drawn inside the game window only; DDLC Plus! replaced file edits with a virtual desktop.
- Debate, no recommendation: No Russian offered a penalty-free skip; The Guardian called it a "cop-out." DDLC's site opens with a content warning.
- Debate: Walt Williams called Spec Ops' controversial scenes "absolutely necessary"; critics split on indictment versus exploitation.
- Debate: critics read Unmanned's boredom as deliberate; Juul faulted September 12th for "cartoonish clearness."
