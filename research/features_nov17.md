# Features from Michael's four games, Nov 17

Proposals for Michael. Nothing here is decided. Mechanics are described in my own words; no characters, art, levels or looks are copied.

The filter for every idea:
- It makes the game more fun or more readable.
- It adds close to zero runtime (the whole game is a short companion piece).
- It says something about the paper: she is fully recorded; whoever aims is never recorded.
- It fits docs/ART_DIRECTION.md: Ash and Gold palette, hard pixels, no glows or tweened scaling, red only for watchers, gold only for wealth, amber only for the lock-on box.

## 1. What each game does, and how it could appear here

### Jump King

1. **Every jump is a commitment.** You hold to charge, pick left, right or up, and let go. Once airborne you cannot steer ([Finger Guns review](https://fingerguns.net/games/2020/06/08/jump-king-review-jump-up-jump-up-and-fall-down/)). There is no charge meter, so you learn the jump by feel ([TheSixthAxis review](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/07/31/jump-king-review/)).
   - Why it works: a jump you cannot correct makes every landing earned, and every miss your own fault. The store page puts it as "your only enemy is yourself" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1061090/Jump_King/)).
   - Here: keep her air control and double jump (Michael asked for them). Add weight in one place only: while she carries her brother's bag in ep4, the double jump is gone and her air control drops by half. The run out of the bedroom becomes a run of committed jumps. Theme: what you carry for someone else makes you slower to escape.

2. **A fall costs ground, not a life.** The king never dies; he dusts himself off. A miss can drop you one ledge, a few screens, or all the way down ([Finger Guns](https://fingerguns.net/games/2020/06/08/jump-king-review-jump-up-jump-up-and-fall-down/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jump_King)).
   - Why it works: Bennett Foddy, on his own climbing game, says progress you have built up and can lose is what gives a skill game stakes. Sliding back "escapes your grasp more than if you just restart" ([Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designer-interview-the-aesthetics-of-frustration-in-i-getting-over-it-i-)).
   - Here: this is the "house hurts, watchers kill" rule from the design pass, made physical. In one tall room, a missed jump drops her to the ledge below and costs ground and maybe one HP. Only the satellite erases her. The fear of falling and the fear of the sky become two different feelings.

3. **The camera cuts screen by screen.** The tower is a stack of fixed screens; a bad jump sends you "back one or two screens" ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/07/31/jump-king-review/)).
   - Why it works: each screen is one readable puzzle. The cut down to a screen you already beat is the punishment you feel in your stomach.
   - Here: in the tall room, the view snaps between fixed screens instead of scrolling. Each screen has a corner label in device type: CAM 01, CAM 02, CAM 03. Falling reads as the show cutting back to an old feed. She moves from camera to camera; there is no frame she is not in.

4. **Sound does the work music would.** No music; you hear the world, your boots and the thud of hitting the ground ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/07/31/jump-king-review/)).
   - Why it works: silence makes each landing loud, so a bad landing hurts before you see where you are.
   - Here: music ducks out in vertical rooms. Landings get three sounds that match the fall damage tiers (soft step, thud with dust, crack with stagger). The player learns the height rules by ear.

5. **The game counts your jumps and your falls.** Its achievements are for 100 falls, 1,000 falls, 1,000 jumps and finishing without a fall ([Steam achievements](https://steamcommunity.com/id/maxmatthisdaniel/stats/1061090/achievements)).
   - Why it works: counting failure turns it into a badge instead of a shame.
   - Here: count her jumps and falls per episode and print them on the episode's paperwork (item 5 in section 2). The show keeps exact numbers on her. It keeps none on who aims.

### Geometry Dash (and Super Meat Boy on the same idea)

1. **Death sends you back to the start at once.** Any hit restarts the level ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_Dash)). Super Meat Boy's makers listed quick restarts and short levels as what made a hard game feel rewarding ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Meat_Boy)).
   - Why it works: when retrying costs under a second, death is information, not a penalty.
   - Here: after her first death, the next take starts in under half a second. No fade to black, no card. Theme: the show does not stop for her.

2. **The attempt number is always in your face.** The counter climbs every try; the session count shows on the end screen ([spilled.gg](https://spilled.gg/geometry-dash-player-totalgd-unnerfed-sakupen-circles-attempts)).
   - Why it works: the number is a joke, a dare and a record at once. Players screenshot it.
   - Here: the take number is painted into the world at her start spot, like tape on a studio floor: TAKE 14. It replaces the 1.5 s lower-third card. Her bodies from earlier takes lie past it. The game already treats retries as canon; this makes the count part of the set.

3. **Levels are built to the song.** Music lines up with the level's design ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_Dash)); the store page calls it rhythm-based ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/322170/Geometry_Dash/)).
   - Why it works: a beat lets you predict hazards without reading anything.
   - Here: searchlight sweeps, flash camera clicks and laser cycles fire on the episode music's beat. The show is broadcast to a schedule, so its traps keep time. Players learn timing by humming, not by reading a page.

4. **Progress is a number, and your best is kept.** You see how far you got as a percentage. (Widely known; I could not open a primary page for this. The Geometry Dash wiki returned HTTP 402.)
   - Why it works: even a failed try gives a small win ("new best") and a goal for the next one.
   - Here: a one-pixel bar in the caption strip fills as she crosses the episode. A red tick marks each spot she died. A brief "BEST" stamp shows when she passes her old best.

5. **Practice is allowed but does not count.** Practice mode lets you place checkpoints; that progress is kept apart and earns no rewards ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_Dash)).
   - Why it works: weak players still see the whole level; strong players still have something to prove.
   - Here: story mode (immunity) is the practice mode. Keep it fully open for the class, but its paperwork is stamped REHEARSAL instead of a rank. Nobody is shamed; the show just does not air it.

6. **Three hidden coins per level, off the main line** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_Dash)).
   - Why it works: a fixed small number ("3") tells you how much is hidden, so looking feels finite.
   - Here: each episode shows 3 empty slots for the clues already tagged in the design pass. They fill on the paperwork. Theme: the only evidence anyone collects about the watchers is what she picks up herself.

### Jetpack Joyride

1. **One input, short sessions.** It was built for "ad break play", short enough to play during a TV commercial, with one button so new players can join ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-depth-in-simplicity-the-making-of-i-jetpack-joyride-i-)).
   - Why it works: short, dense runs suit people who are not gamers. That is Michael's class.
   - Here: this game literally is TV. Hold each episode to about 60 to 90 s for a player who knows it. Optional idea: the moment between an episode and a satellite batch is a 1 s "WE'LL BE RIGHT BACK" bumper, which also hides loading.

2. **Three missions, always on.** Three small goals run at once, such as near misses or passing a number of scientists; finishing them gives stars and levels ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetpack_Joyride)). A design breakdown notes some take seconds and some take many runs, so one is always within reach ([Adrian Crook](https://adriancrook.com/?p=4709)).
   - Why it works: every run, even a bad one, can finish something.
   - Here: the show's three asks per episode, written as a slip from the kids. Example for the gallery: "GET SEEN BY THE PORTRAIT", "CLOSE CALL WITH THE LASER", "BREAK SOMETHING GOLD". They are optional and never block her. The turn: doing them is good for the show, not for her. Later asks drift toward her harm ("LAND A FALL ON CAMERA").

3. **Near misses are rewarded.** Grazing a hazard counts toward missions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetpack_Joyride)).
   - Why it works: it rewards skill and risk without a new system.
   - Here: when she passes within 2 px of a laser or a light's edge, a short audience "ooh" plays (sound only) and a counter ticks for the paperwork. The audience enjoying her danger is the point of the paper's last part.

4. **The results screen is fast and one tap restarts** ([Adrian Crook](https://adriancrook.com/?p=4709)).
   - Why it works: the summary never stands between you and the next try.
   - Here: the episode paperwork shows for under 2 s and any key moves on.

5. **Vehicles break the pace** ([Adrian Crook](https://adriancrook.com/?p=4709)).
   - Why it works: a short change of control is a rest that still feels like play.
   - Here: one short ride per episode, built from props that exist: the chandelier swing (ep2), the dumbwaiter or laundry chute drop (ep3), the collapsing slab (ep4). Three to five seconds each, no new art.

6. **Coins line the path.** You collect them as you fly, and the slot machine at the end turns the run into a prize ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetpack_Joyride)).
   - Why it works: a line of pickups shows the route and rewards a risky line.
   - Here: gold is the only collectible, because gold means wealth: rings, coins, cufflinks lying in the ash along the best line, some on the risky one. A counter shows on the paperwork. Late reveal, backstage: a PROPS DEPARTMENT sheet lists exactly how many were placed, per episode. The trail was bait. It led her where the cameras were.

### Papers, Please

1. **Check a document against the rules, find what is wrong.** Each day you compare papers to the current rules and admit or deny ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)). Pope built the game from noticing the cross-checks real inspectors do and turning them into play ([Game Developer, Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-lucas-pope-s-i-papers-please-i-)).
   - Why it works: spotting a mismatch is a satisfying puzzle that takes seconds, and it makes you complicit in whatever the rules serve.
   - Here: each satellite batch opens with one REQUEST form on the desk. The player checks it against today's rule and finds the problem (no case number; OPERATOR blank; a target description that matches her). This is the paper's Part III made playable: an informal request with no source.

2. **The rules change each day.** New rules arrive in a bulletin and the job gets harder ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)). Pope said fitting rule changes, story beats and events into a sensible sequence was the hardest part, and that players ignored rules announced while someone was talking ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-)).
   - Why it works: the routine stays the same while the ground under it moves. That unease is the story.
   - Here: one-line BULLETIN at the top of each batch, shown when nobody talks. The rules erode instead of growing: batch 1 "Requests need a case number." Batch 2 "Case number optional." Batch 3 "Operator field not required." Batch 4 "All requests pre-approved." The player feels safeguards disappear by doing less checking each time.

3. **Stamps.** You stamp to admit or deny ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)).
   - Why it works: a loud, physical action makes a small choice feel heavy.
   - Here: two stamps, APPROVED and DENIED, in red because they are the watchers'. K or a click. The stamp drops in 2 frames, thunks, shakes the desk 1 px. DENIED lands too, but a reply slides in: "DENIAL NOTED. REQUEST PROCESSED." Refusal is recorded and ignored.

4. **Mistakes come back as citations.** A citation prints shortly after the person leaves, and penalties cut your pay ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)). The family is "a few dots with some text," left vague so players project their own ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-)).
   - Why it works: the system answers you in its own dry voice, and a tiny status screen carries more feeling than a cutscene.
   - Here: a citation slip prints when the operator is slow or denies: "CITATION: DELAY IN SERVICE." No money system. The weight is that the slips stack up on the desk across batches and stay there.

5. **Monotony and a drab, clunky interface on purpose.** Pope chose muted colors and stripped detail for the mood ([Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-lucas-pope-s-i-papers-please-i-)); critics tied the clunky interface to life under a police state ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)). Pope also says simple assets are what let him keep the nuances ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-)).
   - Why it works: sameness is felt, and it makes the one change stand out.
   - Here: every episode's paperwork uses the same form, same layout, same fields. Only numbers change. When something new finally appears on a form (OPERATOR filled in, backstage: "PLAYER 2"), it lands hard.

6. **Small choices add up to many endings.** Twenty endings come from your actions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)). Days are labeled by newspaper headlines, which also helps you find where to replay ([Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=2176)). The game gives time back when story costs time ([Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=2176)). One review reads the whole government as a panopticon felt through the sound of a printed citation ([GDC narrative review, Weinstein](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/GameNarrativeReview/Adam%20Weinstein%20U%20of%20U%202020%20PapersPlease.pdf)).
   - Why it works: you never see a big choice coming; the ending is a sum of stamps.
   - Here: each batch's stamp is saved to the story state. Denying all four counts toward the LOOK BACK ending from the design pass. Chapters in the title menu get one headline each from a Moon paper ("CH 07 RATINGS UP 40%").

### Juice, from the two classic talks on it

Both talks show the same small game with and without effects. "Juice It or Lose It" (Jonasson and Purho, 2012) adds stretch on impact, color change on hit, particles, a trail and screen shake. "The Art of Screenshake" (Nijman, 2013) adds bigger bullets, camera kick, knockback, sleep (a short pause on a hit), permanence (shells stay on the floor) and more bass in sound ([lecture slides listing both talks](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/GxD-10-Game-Feel.pdf); [Game Developer on Nijman's talk](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)). Our bodies that stay are already Nijman's permanence. The art rules forbid tweened scaling and glows, so every effect below is done with sprite frames, pixels and sound.

## 2. The 12 best additions, ranked

Runtime is for a player who knows the game. "+0" means it adds no time.

**1. Instant takes with the take number on the floor**
- Sees and does: after the first death, she is back at the start in under 0.5 s. No fade, no card. The floor at the start spot reads TAKE 14 in tape, and a slate clack plays.
- Where: every side and top-down episode, and the fight.
- Theme: the show never stops for her; the count is part of the set.
- Size: S. Runtime: saves time (about 1 to 2 s per death).

**2. Landing and hit juice, inside the art rules**
- Sees and does: a 1-frame squash sprite on landing (10 x 12 instead of 8 x 14) and a 1-frame stretch on jump. Square dust puffs (2 to 4 px) on landings and wall jumps. Three landing sounds by height tier. 2 frames of sleep plus 1 to 2 px shake only on HP loss and strikes. Hurt blink on the 1 s invulnerability. No shake on normal landings.
- Where: core/side.js and core/fx.js (reduced motion and the flash cap still apply).
- Theme: her body has weight; the house is physical. The strike stays the biggest effect in the game.
- Size: S. Runtime: +0.

**3. Request inspection in each satellite batch**
- Sees and does: before the globe maze, one REQUEST form on the desk. Hover or tab through 4 fields; one is wrong under today's rule. Stamp APPROVED or DENIED. Either way the maze runs.
- Where: batches 1 to 4, in each batch's skin (terminal, ground station, flat map, game show). The form gets friendlier as the screens do; by batch 4 it is a cartoon entry ticket.
- Theme: the paper's informal requests with no source and no record. The player does the paperwork that lets the strike happen.
- Size: M. Runtime: about +10 s per batch. Pay it back by capping each globe maze at about 90 s.

**4. The bulletin: one rule that changes each batch**
- Sees and does: one line at the top of the desk. Rules erode: case number required, then optional, then operator not required, then all requests pre-approved.
- Where: satellite batches, on top of item 3.
- Theme: safeguards disappear one notice at a time. Players feel it as less to check.
- Size: S (text plus one field check). Runtime: +0 (read while the form slides in).

**5. Episode paperwork: the continuity report and its stamp**
- Sees and does: at each episode exit, a form drops for under 2 s: TAKES, TIME, FALLS, JUMPS, CLOSE CALLS, GOLD, CLUES 1/3, and OPERATOR: (none). A red stamp grades it: PRIME TIME, DAYTIME, LATE NIGHT or RERUN, from takes and time. Story mode stamps REHEARSAL. Any key moves on.
- Where: end of ep1 to ep4 and the fight; one final stack of all five forms before the playroom.
- Theme: the record runs one way. Every number about her is exact; the operator line is always blank. For the class walkthrough it also gives Michael a clear beat to talk over.
- Size: S to M. Runtime: about +2 s per episode, skippable.

**6. Broadcast bar with death ticks and a best mark**
- Sees and does: a 1 px bar along the top of the caption strip fills as she crosses the episode. Red ticks mark where she died. Passing her old best flashes BEST once in the strip.
- Where: side episodes (top-down east wing uses distance to the exit).
- Theme: the show measures her progress like airtime. The red ticks are the watchers' marks on her.
- Size: S. Runtime: +0.

**7. The cut: every take at once**
- Sees and does: on finishing an episode, 3 s of every take replayed together as faint ash-grey ghosts, all running the same room, most dropping where she died. One live figure makes it. Skippable.
- Where: end of each side episode, before the paperwork.
- Theme: the clones, the bodies and the archive in one image. The show has every second of her.
- Precedent: Super Meat Boy shows all attempts at once after a level; its makers felt it "transformed death into a form of reward" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Meat_Boy)).
- Size: M (record her position each frame per take; draw with ghost frames). Runtime: about +3 s per episode, skippable.

**8. Gold bait trail**
- Sees and does: gold pieces in the ash along the best line, some on risky lines (near a light's edge, above a gap). A soft chime and a counter on the paperwork. Backstage, a PROPS DEPARTMENT sheet lists how many were placed per episode, more than any player found.
- Where: ep1 to ep4; the reveal sheet in ep5b.
- Theme: wealth is the lure; the trail led into the cameras.
- Size: S to M. Runtime: +0 (on the path).

**9. The show's three asks**
- Sees and does: a slip at the start of each episode, three short lines in a child's hand. Ticks fill in as they happen. Optional. Example, ep1: "GET CAUGHT IN A SEARCHLIGHT AND LIVE", "CLOSE CALL x3", "TAKE THE GOLD BY THE FOUNTAIN". Later asks ask for her falls and deaths.
- Where: ep1 to ep4 and the fight; results on the paperwork.
- Theme: the audience as client. Doing what the viewers want feels like play and slowly turns into self-harm. Can count toward ROUND TWO (complicity) in the endings.
- Size: M. Runtime: +0 (the slip shows during the start, or only on the paperwork).

**10. Hazards on the beat**
- Sees and does: searchlight sweeps, laser cycles and flash camera clicks happen on the music's beat (for example every 4 beats). A short tick plays one beat before each.
- Where: grounds, gallery, bedroom and the fight's traps.
- Theme: the broadcast runs on a schedule; its traps keep time like a clock in a studio.
- Size: M (retime hazards to a shared beat clock in core/music.js). Runtime: +0. Makes hazards readable without any text.

**11. The camera stack: one Jump King style climb**
- Sees and does: one tall room per game, likely the gallery's stair hall. The view snaps between fixed screens labeled CAM 01 to CAM 03. Ledges are spaced so a miss drops one ledge (under the 1 HP fall line) rather than killing. Music drops out; only boots and landings.
- Where: ep2, as the gallery's vertical section. It reuses wall jump and double jump.
- Theme: she moves from feed to feed. Falling is the show cutting back. The house costs ground; only the sky kills.
- Size: M to L (layout work). Runtime: about +20 s. The one content item on this list; cut first if time is short.

**12. The heavy bag**
- Sees and does: carrying her brother's bag out of the bedroom, she loses the double jump and half her air control. Landings thud louder. The collapse run becomes committed jumps.
- Where: ep4, the bag pull and the collapse run.
- Theme: what she carries for him slows her escape; the weight is grief made physical.
- Size: S (a flag in side.js). Runtime: +0.

Honorable mentions, not ranked: 1 s "WE'LL BE RIGHT BACK" bumpers between episodes and batches; citation slips that pile up on the satellite desk; Moon-paper headlines labeling each chapter in the title menu; near-miss "ooh" sound (folds into items 5 and 9).

## 3. Where the current design fights these lessons, and the fix

1. **Death is still slow the first time and wordy every time.** core/death.js plays the full strike, then waits 0.42 s, fades 260 ms, and restarts. A caption names the cause on every death, a "Space skips" note appears, and a TAKE card shows for 1.5 s after the restart.
   - Fix: full strike on the first death per cause only. After that, a short strike and restart in under 0.5 s without a key press. Death caption once per cause, then never. Drop the "Space skips" note after the first time. Replace the TAKE card with the floor mark (item 1).

2. **How-to pages pause the game.** core/panels.js pauses the scene for each instruction panel. Geometry Dash and Jetpack Joyride teach inside play; Papers, Please puts rules on a slip you glance at.
   - Fix: how-to becomes a NOTICE slip in the caption strip, a key cap plus at most 3 words, with no pause. The first hazard of each kind is placed where failing it is cheap.

3. **Too many words overall.** Narration on every look, three-line captions, bubbles, how-to pages, death captions, and satellite text. Pope warns that players miss rules shown while someone talks ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-)).
   - Fix: one line per look, as the art direction says. Never show a rule and a spoken line at once. Move numbers and facts onto paperwork the player can choose to read (items 4 and 5). Put hazard timing into sound and beat (item 10), not text.

4. **Falling can kill.** Fall damage kills from 176 px. That breaks the design pass's own rule (what watches her kills; the house hurts) and Jump King's lesson that falls cost ground.
   - Fix: falls cost at most 2 HP and a stagger. Death needs HP at 0 from several house hits, or a watcher. Rooms are laid out so a miss costs ground (item 11).

5. **Lives fight instant restarts.** Five lives that send her back to the episode start change little, since every take already starts there. The penalty is mostly an intro card and lost time.
   - Fix (Michael's call): running out of takes does not reset anything. It changes the paperwork stamp to OVER SCHEDULE and the show's tone. Lives stay visible as TAKES LEFT; they cost dignity, not runtime.

6. **Satellite batches could get long.** The design pass suggests 2 to 3 min per globe maze across four batches, which is up to 12 minutes of puzzle in a short piece. Papers, Please gives time back when story takes time ([Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=2176)).
   - Fix: target 60 to 90 s per maze, plus about 10 s of form and stamp. The existing "N solves it" at 90 s stays. Batch 4 can be the shortest, since the reveal carries it.

7. **Long stretches with no stakes.** The Moon city has no deaths and a long walk. Rests are good, but Jetpack Joyride's rests still have play in them.
   - Fix: keep the city calm but under about 40 s of walking. Give it the gold trail (item 8) and one ask ("WAVE AT A CAMERA"), so walking still scores on the paperwork.

8. **Restarts may replay set dressing.** If the episode's lower third, music intro or how-to page plays again on scene.restart, the retry is slower than it looks. (Check each episode.)
   - Fix: on a retry, skip every intro element. Music keeps playing or restarts on the beat so the hazard rhythm (item 10) still lines up.

9. **Juice must not break the art rules.** Geometry Dash and Jetpack Joyride lean on bright color and glow. The art direction bans glows, gradients and tweened scaling.
   - Fix: all juice is sprite frames, square particles, sound and 1 to 2 px shake (item 2). Color stays rare. Red stays only for watchers, so the stamp, the death ticks and the asks' tick marks are red and nothing else is.
